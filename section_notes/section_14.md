## 1. Trend
### 1.1 What Is a Trend?
- "Trend" can be translated as "fashion". When something is trending on social media, it is the recent fashion.
- A market that "has a trend" or is a "trending market" is going in one direction and following it: price either rising or falling.

### 1.2 Three Kinds of Market
1. **Uptrend** (bullish; price increasing).
2. **Downtrend** (bearish; price decreasing).
3. **Sideways / consolidating.**

- Recall from the previous section: **bull** = up/increase, **bear** = down/decrease.

### 1.3 Highs and Lows
- A **High** is a price point that, compared with the price points beside it, goes up and then pulls back. It is where a market that was rising turns down.
- A **Low** is a price point lower than its neighbors, where price bounces back up (reverses upward).
- No matter how strong the price is, whether rising a lot or falling a lot, it does **not** move in a straight line. It zigzags: it rises a bit, drops a bit, rises again. There is no perfectly straight market. In a rising market it rises, falls back a bit, and rises again.
- In a trending market we look at whether highs and lows are higher or lower than the previous ones.

### 1.4 Uptrend (Bullish)
- Each new high is **higher** than the previous high: **Higher High (HH)**.
- Each new low is **higher** than the previous low: **Higher Low (HL)**.
- A market that makes higher highs and higher lows is an **uptrend**.

### 1.5 Downtrend (Bearish)
- Each new high is **lower** than the previous high: **Lower High (LH)**.
- Each new low is **lower** than the previous low: **Lower Low (LL)**.
- A market that makes lower highs and lower lows is a **bearish market / bearish trend**.

### 1.6 Sideways / Consolidating Market
- Highs and lows form at **roughly equal levels**. They are not exactly equal, but relatively equal. Higher highs and higher lows don't form.
- **Choppy market** (a separate case): it is not trending (not up, not down) and not going sideways at equal levels either. Price action is haphazard: a high forms, then a low, and highs and lows come at different levels (sometimes a higher high, then a lower low, and so on), so the direction is hard to identify.

### 1.7 Which Markets to Trade
- **Trending markets** are good for trading and opening positions. They are easier to predict: with a trend established, we enter to follow it.
- **Consolidating / sideways markets** still give opportunities.
- **Choppy markets** should be **avoided**, because they are very hard to predict. Trading markets give good opportunities. In a choppy market, refrain from trading until the market settles.

## 2. Support and Resistance (S&R)
- A very important concept that will keep being mentioned. Its name or form may change as we refine it, but support and resistance is basic trading knowledge to know first.

### 2.1 Support
- A place where price **gets support**. A cheap price comes down, and when it reaches some level it doesn't go lower and **bounces back**. The price fails to fall further there.
- When price reaches it again, it gives us the expectation that price may bounce up from here.
- Illustration: price came down and reversed up at a line; it came again at about the same place and reversed up; it touched again and reversed up. That is support.

### 2.2 Resistance
- Price was rising, then at some level it breaks its climb, can't go higher, and returns down. When it returns to that place again, it can't go up again. That is **resistance**: the place that withstands the price.
- Illustration: price went down, came back up and reversed down at one point; again, it reversed down. Resistance, because the price fails to break upward.

### 2.3 Why We Want Them
- When price reaches a support or resistance point, we assume "OK, it may react here". It won't just pass: it may reverse or bounce.

### 2.4 It Is a Zone, Not a Single Point
- Support and resistance are **zones** (a general area). Price comes and respects an area, not one exact point where it "bounces exactly".
- A drawn line is only an approximation for easier understanding. Don't think the exact line will be respected.

### 2.5 Respect vs Break
- **Respected**: price comes to a support/resistance point or zone and **reverses** (up or down).
- **Broken**: price goes through the zone and carries on. That S&R point was "broken".
- What we must learn to distinguish is whether, when a support or resistance exists, it will **break or hold**. A few tricks follow (more in future sessions).

### 2.6 Tricks for Judging Strength
1. **Higher timeframe vs lower timeframe**
   - Support/resistance on **higher timeframes** has a greater chance of being respected (not broken) than on lower timeframes.
   - On lower timeframes, structure often doesn't hold and breaks quickly.
   - As we move toward higher timeframes, the chance of respect rises.
2. **Tested and respected before**
   - If there has been a support/resistance point that was tested and respected earlier, then the next time price arrives its **validity increases**: "OK, this is a good place, price might reverse here".
   - Each time a point is respected, its strength grows.
3. **Don't trade on resistance alone**
   - We won't open or close a trade just because we see a resistance. These are points where we **adapt and look to buy**, and later we will see how to confirm.
   - A tested support adds to the strength. 

### 2.7 Break vs Fake-Out
- **Break**: price breaks the S&R point and keeps going.
- **Fake-out**: price slightly breaks S&R, then returns and respects it. A small example is on the chart, and the module has a clearer example.
- It is easy to see on past price, but on the **live market** it is hard to tell. If price reaches support and slightly passes it, is it a break or a fake-out? That is hard to know in real time.
- **One tip: momentum.**
  - If price arrives with **momentum** (high speed and good **volume**), it has a chance of breaking the S&R point.
  - If it does **not** arrive with momentum, and especially if the volume before it was **decreasing**, the S&R point is likely to be respected.
- **Volume** means how many sellers or buyers there are.
  - At a support point, there are many sellers and the market comes with volume. If the volume falls and sellers decrease as it reaches support, the **buyers have taken control**, a hint that the support will be respected.
  - But if it comes with volume and momentum, with sellers adding more and more, that support likely won't be respected and may be broken, or its validity is reduced. 

### 2.8 Role Reversal
- Another concept: **role reversal**. Many trading principles work this way.
- A support that was broken can **act as resistance** when price returns to it (the former support place becomes resistance), and vice versa.
- On the chart: support was broken, price came back, and the old support acted as resistance. This will appear in other contexts too; it is one principle on the resistance side.

## 3. What Can Act as Support/Resistance
To look for places that could be support or resistance, we use the following (the simplest is first):

1. **Previous highs / previous lows** (the first and easiest).
   - A previous high serves as a resistance point when the next high forms.
   - Previous lows serve as support in the same way.
2. **Psychological levels**: round numbers, e.g., **1.00** or **1.5500**.
   - **EUR/USD around 2022** reached parity (1 EUR = 1 USD). That was a psychological support. The pair kept falling, reached 1.00, and struggled to go below it. It bounced around there, paused for a while, and has since come back. 
   - **Gold**: round levels like **3000, 4000, 4500, 5000**. Traders treat them as psychological levels and expect a reversal there, and many take trades on that basis, so price often respects them. They can serve as support or resistance.
3. **Trend lines**: connect the lows (in an uptrend) or the highs (in a downtrend). A trend line can serve as support/resistance. A brief note now, with more on the concept later.
   - **Uptrend**: price is going up, so we wait for the market from **below**. Connecting the higher lows gives an **uptrend line**. If price returns, touches the line, and bounces (respects it), it continues. The line can act as **support**.
   - **Downtrend**: connecting the lower highs gives a **downtrend line**. It can act as **resistance**. Price came, touched the line, and the third time it respected it.
   - We can also draw boxes/zones on both sides of the line.
4. **Moving averages**: they can serve as support/resistance. The next class is on indicators, where moving averages are covered in more depth. **Fibonacci** can also be thought of as an indicator or trading tool, to be covered in the future.
5. **Supply and demand zones**: another concept, covered in a longer session. Supply and demand zones can serve as support/resistance too.

## 1. Market Structure 
- **Market structure** is the way price moves on a chart; it shows the direction the market is going.
- Recognizing it is the **first and most important step** to becoming a skilled trader.
- It helps a trader **describe what price is currently doing before trying to predict what it may do next.**
- Three phases: Uptrend = bullish market, Sideways = range-bound market, Downtrend = bearish market.

## 2. Support and Resistance meaning
- Support = a **floor** that holds the price up; it shows **buyers stepping in** and willing to buy at that level.
- Resistance = a **ceiling** the price cannot break easily; it shows **sellers are active** and pushing price lower.

## 3. Real Break and Retest 
  1. **Fakeout:** price briefly above resistance, then returns below.
  2. **Real break:** strong close above the level.
  3. **Retest:** price comes back and the old resistance acts as **new support**.
  4. **Continuation:** price moves higher.

## 4. Strength example
- A support level on the **4H chart** is more powerful than one on the **15-minute chart**.

## 5. Previous Highs and Lows reasoning 
- A previous **high** = area where price stopped rising and **sellers became active**.
- A previous **low** = area where price stopped falling and **buyers became active**.
- When price returns to these areas, traders **watch for another reaction**.

## 6. Round Number examples
- Examples: **1.1000, 3000.00, 100.00**.
- Many traders place buy or sell orders at these levels, causing strong reactions.
- Especially common in **forex and commodities** markets.

## 7. Trendlines 
- Diagonal lines drawn along **swing highs or swing lows**.
- They can **change position as time and price move forward** (dynamic).

## 8. Moving Averages
- **Smooth out price movements** and often act as **dynamic** support or resistance.
- Price may pull back to the **50-day or 200-day** moving average and then continue higher.

## 9. Fibonacci Retracement 
- Levels show where price might **pause or reverse during a pullback**.
- Commonly used: **38.2%, 50%, 61.8%**.
- Drawn **between a high and a low**.

## 10. Supply and Demand Zones
- **Demand zone:** where strong **buying** pushed prices up; can act as **support**.
- **Supply zone:** where strong **selling** drove prices down; can act as **resistance**.
- They are **wider areas** where price moved sharply in the past.

## 10. Key Takeaway 
- Begin by identifying the market structure, then mark the areas where price has **repeatedly reacted**.
- Support and resistance are **not guaranteed reversal points**, but they help traders **organize the chart and prepare for possible price reactions**.
