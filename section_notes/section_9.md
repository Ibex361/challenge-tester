# Section 9: Trading Account Types
*(Extracted from video & module)*

## 1. Two Broad Account Categories
Trading accounts types are: **Demo** and **Real** accounts.

### Demo Account
- it's a **practice account**, Uses **virtual (fake) money** — cannot be withdrawn — but you set an amount and practice on the market as if it were real.
- Example of demo account: depositing "$100" into a demo account means you have $100 to trade with freely — you can grow it or lose it, but any profit made is **not real, withdrawable**.

### Real Account
- An account where you deposit **actual money**, and can genuinely profit or genuinely lose.
- It can be withdrawn.

### Key Point: Demo and Real Accounts Look the Same But Are Very Different for Traders
Stated explicitly that although both accounts show the same market, they have two types of differences:

#### 1st difference: Emotional Differences
- Described as something you don't fully appreciate until you experience it yourself — especially as beginners.
- In trading, one will discover behaviors/traits one wasn't aware before.
- **On a Demo account**: there's no fear, since it isn't your real money — even if you lose. You can trade with confidence, simply following your plan. A profit doesn't bring the same excitement either — either way, there are no strong emotions.
- Practical benefit: because there's no fear, it's easier to **stick to your predetermined trading plan/strategy exactly as written** This has two components:
  1. **No fear** — when a trade starts moving into a small loss, you don't panic and manually close.
  2. **No greed** — greed is one of the **big issues in trading**. Example given: a trader takes a certain lot size, and if that trade makes $100 profit, immediately starts thinking "if I use a bigger lot size (2 or 3 lots), I'd have made $200–300 instead" — leading them to increase lot size on the next trades. This mindset is described as something that affects **many traders** and leads **many to significant losses**. On a Demo account, greed largely doesn't exist, because you're just testing whether your strategy works or not — without any financial stake.

 Many people who perform well on Demo accounts start losing in Real accounts — primarily because of emotional difference; 
 
 Traders who traded calmly and confidently on Demo often find themselves 
 - panicking, getting disturbed, or closing trades manually before their planned levels once real money is on the line.

#### 2nd difference: Market Difference
Market related difference between demo & real are:
1. **Slippage**
   - Definition: when you place an order (especially during high-volatility hours), by the time the order actually executes (a fraction of a second later), the price may have moved from what you saw — the order fills at a different price than requested.
   - This can vary — it can occur across different volatility periods, potentially causing your order to execute at a different price/time than you clicked.
   - **On Real accounts**: slippage is a real phenomenon that can occur — it depends on broker quality; but even with a good broker, some slippage can occur when the market moves very fast.
   - **On Demo accounts**: slippage generally does **not** occur, because there's no real contract or fund behind your order or a deal that gets executed— the platform is just simulating execution for you.

2. **Spread variability**
   - **On Real accounts**: spread is not constant — it's variable and can change hour to hour, especially widening during the Asian session (as discussed in the prior section), when the market is closing (rollover time, when the trading day changes), or during other specific market conditions.
   - **On Demo accounts**: spread tends to be relatively stable on Demo. Variable spread is the reason many scalpers (fast in-and-out traders) perform well on Demo but fail on real accounts.

Summary of the two differences: (1) Emotional differences, and (2) how the markets themselves behave differently.
## 2. Recommended Approach / Process
The process a trader should follow is: 
1. get proper **knowledge** first →
2. then move to a **Demo account** to acquire skill →
3. once your strategy works well on Demo and you're getting comfortable with the market. NB: this doesn't mean you are pro now, you need real account experience so the next step is
4. move to a **Real account** with some capital, specifically to see how the real market conditions and your own emotional factors play out →
5. then gradually **scale up** your capital as you improve, eventually managing larger capital.

## 3. Real Account Types Offered by Brokers
Note stated: available account types can differ by broker. The reference video uses **Exness**as the example, while noting the same general categories exist across brokers.

### A. Standard-Level Accounts
Two variants exist under "Standard":

**1. Regular Standard Account**
- Prepared for the masses. Suited for beginner-to-moderate level traders.
- Market conditions here are described as "moderate" — good spreads (varies by broker), slippage might not be excessive, good spread variance.
**2. Standard Cent (Micro/Cent) Account**
- Called "Standard Cent Account" on Exness; other brokers may call it "Micro Cent Account."
- Meaning: your account balance is denominated in **cents** rather than dollars. 
- Example given on cent account: depositing $10 shows up as if it were 1000 cents in the account — profits and losses are similarly stated in cents.
- Lot sizing in cent account: what would be **0.01 lot on a Standard account becomes 1 lot on a Cent account**. 
- On most brokers, the smallest lot size on a Standard account is 0.01 lot (one micro lot. If you want to hold a size smaller than that— using Cent accounts, you can effectively go down to sizes smaller than a single micro lot. 
- Example given: on Gold, where 0.01 lot might already represent a fairly large pip value for a small account, a Cent account lets you size down much further — e.g., holding "0.5 lot" on a Cent account effectively means holding half of one micro lot on standard account, and you can scale down to the equivalent of 0.01 of a standard account's micro lot (i.e., 1/100th of a micro lot).
- **Upside of cent accounts**: it is a good way to get **real account experience** with very little financial exposure.
- **Downside of cent accounts**: In general, it gives you the worst market conditions, what this exactly mean is:
  1. Spread tends to be **wider**.
  2. More slippage
  3. Variance in spread is greater.

**In summary**: Even though the market conditions in Cent accounts are the worst, Cent accounts are good to get real account experience. If you deposit a $10 deposit in a standard account, it can be lost within one or two trades quickly if care isn't taken, even you use a standard micro lot. But if you use Cent account, you can open relatively many trades (because it allows you to open small sized trades), allowing you to build valuable data/experience.

### B. Professional-Level Accounts
There are two types of pro accouns:

**1. ECN Account** (the most professional)
- With an ECN account, your order goes **directly to the network**, rather than being filled internally by the broker — meaning there's **no spread** for ECN accouns.
- Brokers that offer **true ECN accounts**: **IC Markets** and **Pepperstone**.
- **Trade-off**: instead of a spread, the broker charges a **commission** — approximately **$7 per 1 standard lot**, i.e., $3.50 on entry + $3.50 on exit per lot.
- Scalpers prefer ECN accounts, because they don't have spread. So you can choose them based on your trading style.

**2. Professional ("Pro") Account**
- Not an ECN account, but still has **very minimal spread and minimal slippage**, with low spread variance.
- **Downside of Professional (Pro) accounts**: higher minimum deposit requirement. See below:
- Example of higher deposit requirement: in Exness, minimum deposit for **Regular/Cent accounts = $10**. Minimum deposit for **Professional-level (Pro) accounts**: previously **$500**, **currently $200** — i.e., you must deposit starting from $200 to access Pro account.

- **Recommendation**: as your capital grows through trading, it is **strongly recommended** to move to Professional-level accounts (specifically: ECN for scalpers, Pro account for regular day traders)

## 4. Overall Recommendation and Realistic Expectations
- **Recommended path restated**: begin with a **Demo account** first to build good skills/data, then move to a **Real account** — and when moving to Real, it's normal/expected that **most people's first real accounts will lose money** — this is common, so start small; there's no need to feel pressured. It's recommended to **start with a Cent account**, since it's flexible, lets you hold real data/experience while the market conditions are somewhat less severe than other real accounts, giving you a gentler entry point.
- Progression recommended: **Demo → small Real (Cent) account → gain experience there, this is less harsh when compared to other real accounts → grow emotionally more resilient over time** (fear/greed don't fully disappear, but control over them improves and grows with experience, rather than vanishing completely) → **gradually move up to Professional-level accounts** as you improve and your capital grows.
