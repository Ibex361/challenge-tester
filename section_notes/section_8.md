# BirrForex Academy — Section 8: Trading Time Sessions
*(Full content extracted from video transcript, in order)*

## 1. Setup — Forex Is Open 24/5, But Behaves Differently by Hour
- Forex market is open **24 hours a day, 5 days a week** (business/weekdays). Crypto markets are also open on Saturdays, but the normal Forex market is open **Monday through Friday**.
- Even though the market is open 24 hours, **the market's behavior/character differs from hour to hour**.
- The reason for this: **which traders are active** at any given time. This depends on the time of day for different regions of the world.
- Based on this, the market is generally divided into **three sessions**: **Asia, London, and New York.**

## 2. Important Clarification: These Are NOT Fixed Open/Close Times
- Explicit warning given: don't misunderstand these sessions as if there's a fixed clock time when a session "opens" and "closes," like the **futures market** works (futures are bell-triggered: to open & to close, with defined times).
- **Forex trading is OTC (Over-The-Counter)** —  **decentralized**, with **no single central exchange**, spread across banks and institutions worldwide, connected via the **inter-bank network**.
- but, when we generalize and talk about these sessions, what we're describing is: **"which traders are active during this or that time period."**
- If you search online for "Asian trading session hours," the specific times might vary. What matters is understanding the **overall interval and who is active within it**.

## 3. Asian Trading Session

### Who's active and why
- Represents traders/institutions/banks active in **Asia** — e.g., **Sydney, Tokyo, Hong Kong, Shanghai**, and similar.
- why?: when it's business hours in Asia, it's nighttime in the US, so American banks and institutional traders are not active — but the Asians are.
### Time range of Asian Session 
- Because it covers a broad group of markets, the overall interval is wide: roughly from **our (Ethiopian) nighttime 6 o'clock (12:00 AM) to our midday 6 o'clock (12:00 PM)** — i.e., an approximately 12-hour covering multiple Asian markets that open/close at slightly different times within it.
- Asian sub-market example 1: the **Sydney market** opens around our nighttime 6 o'clock (~12:00 AM) and closes around our morning 3 o'clock (~9:00 AM).
- Asian sub-market example 2: the **Tokyo market** opens around our nighttime 9 o'clock (~3:00 AM) and closes around our morning 6 o'clock (~12:00 PM).
- Combined/generalized as "Asia".

### Characteristics of Asian session
- Generally characterized by **low movement/consolidation** — tends to move **sideways** rather than trending strongly: prices go up a bit, come back down, go up again, staying in defined range, not breaking out.
- The currencies that tend to be **more active** during this session: **Asian currencies**, specifically: **Australian Dollar (AUD), New Zealand Dollar (NZD), and Japanese Yen (JPY)**.
- Historically described as a **low-volatility, slow-moving session** overall.
- **However, a caveat/update given**: this is changing. Asia's financial activity is increasing — good market moves are increasingly happening in it, **volume used to be very low during this session, but is improving now.**
- Despite this improvement, the Asian session is still described as having **less activity compared to London and New York**.
- Practical consequence of lower volume: because volume is lower, **spreads tend to be wider** during this session — lower liquidity leads brokers to widen spreads (to maintain their profit margins).

## 4. London Trading Session

### Who's active and why
- Represents **European banks and European institutions** being highly active.
- Named markets that fall under "London" as a generalized group: **London itself, plus markets like Frankfurt, Paris, Zurich**.

### London time range
- Starts around **our 5 o'clock** (~11:00 AM) and closes around **our nighttime 2 o'clock** (~8:00 PM).

### Characteristics of London session
- Described as the session where **European banks and institutions are very active** — a period of **strong movement**, described as **impulsive** market moves with **decent volume**.
- Because volume is good during London, **spreads tend to be lower/tighter**.
- Typical pattern described: the market often **consolidates during the Asian session**, and then **once London opens, it tends to pick a direction and make a strong "pump"** — described as a common/usual pattern, though not an absolute.

## 5. New York Trading Session

### Who's active and why
- Also called the **"American" session**. Represents US-based markets/institutions.
- Named markets: **New York, Chicago, Toronto** — generalized together as "New York."

### Time range given
- Starts around **our 10 o'clock in the day** (~4:00 PM) and closes at **our 7 o'clock in the night (1 AM).**

### Characteristics of this session
- Also a **very active session** — American banks and American institutions are moving, resulting in **good volume and good/tight spreads**.
- Can either **strongly continue** the directional impulse that started during the London session, **or reverse it entirely** and start a new impulse moving in the opposite direction.

## 6. The London–New York Overlap

- The market is **most active** during the **overlap between London and New York** —  when **both** European and American institutions are active.
- Time range of the overlap: roughly **our 10 o'clock (~4:00 PM) to our nighttime 2 o'clock (~8:00 PM)**.

## 7. "Kill Zones"

- Definition: **Kill Zones** refer to the **first 1–2 hours right after a trading session opens** — the moment institutional traders/banks "sit down for business" and immediately begin opening trades, causing a significant market movement.
- **New York kill zone time**: from around **our 10 o'clock to 12:00 o'clock** (the first ~2 hours after New York opens).
- **London kill zone**:  from around **our 5 o'clock to 7 o'clock** (the first ~2 hours after London opens).
- **Asian kill zone**: also the first 2 hours.
- A "Kill Zone" is typically when the market makes **one large impulsive move**.

## 8. Daylight Saving Time — A Caveat on All These Time Ranges

- **Daylight Saving Time** exists, it affects European and American time zones.
- It roughly runs from **around March to November**. During this period, session times can shift.
- **Example 1**: London's session, normally opening at our 5 o'clock and closing at nighttime 2 o'clock, may shift during Daylight Saving to instead open at our 4 o'clock and close at nighttime 1 o'clock.
- **Example 2:** New York's session, normally opening at our 10 o'clock afternoon and closing at our nighttime 7 o'clock, may shift during Daylight Saving to instead open at our 9 o'clock and close at our nighttime 6 o'clock.
- **Important note**: the Asian markets mostly **do not observe Daylight Saving** — except **Sydney**, which does observe it, and its times may also shift *by roughly an hour accordingly.*
- Restated general advice: don't think of any of these times as an exact minute-by-minute schedule — think of them in terms of the overall interval, since the Forex market is an OTC market.

NB: Ethiopians colloqually start counting the 12 hours of the day from the sunrise (6 AM) and count the rest 12 hours of the night from sunset (6 PM), hence 12 AM & 12 PM are respectively called 6 o'clock midnight and 6 o'clock midday. So if a time is described as local but without AM suffix, consider it in Ethiopian style, so if a time is stated as 4 o'clock in the night, know it is 10 PM (10 PM is 4 hours after sunset). But if there is AM alongside it, consider as it is: 3 AM is then 3 AM, which locally can be described as 9 o'clock in the night (3 AM is 9 hours after sunset).

NB: in the note, our time or local time (without  AM or PM)  refers to the Ethiopian colloquial time explained above. Don't let that confuse you.
